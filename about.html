What we call
Superaccurate AI

In 1997, researchers in Iowa gave volunteers four decks of cards and $2,000 in play money. Two decks would quietly ruin them. By the tenth card, the players' skin began to sweat each time they considered a bad deck. They felt a hunch around card 50. Most could explain it only around card 80.

Knowledge without judgement looks a lot like today's AI. Models can recite the rule and still reach for the wrong deck. At Deccan AI, we teach models the part that arrives before the reasons, the instinct experts build over thousands of hands.

Patients with damage to one patch of the prefrontal cortex never showed that warning. A few worked out the rule perfectly and kept choosing the bad decks anyway.
